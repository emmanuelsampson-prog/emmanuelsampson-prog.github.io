Emmanuel Amissah Sampson is a Master of Urban Spatial Analytics candidate in the Department of City and Regional Planning at the University of Pennsylvania's Stuart Weitzman School of Design, where he is a Stuart Weitzman Scholar. His research asks how the location choices of firms and public facilities create spillovers across urban networks, how those choices shape access to everyday services and health care, and how digital technology shapes markets and access to land and services in African cities.

Before Penn, he spent more than a decade bringing geospatial technology to governments, utilities, mines and donor-funded programmes across Africa: first at Trimble (2015–2023), where he won $85M in competitive bids and secured site permits for national GNSS reference station networks used in land administration, and then as Geospatial Regional Manager for East and Central Africa at Leica Geosystems (Hexagon). Earlier, he co-led value-chain research for the Cocoa Life programme in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire with CARE International.

He holds an MSc in Development Management from the London School of Economics, an MBA with Distinction from Lancaster University, and a BSc in Business Administration with First Class Honours from KNUST, Ghana. He has authored seven journal articles on digital transformation, AI and financial inclusion, and was a Perplexity AI Business Fellow in 2025.

Pharmacy Closures and Access in Philadelphia, 2023–2025

Research report · University of Pennsylvania · November 2026

When Rite Aid closed its Philadelphia pharmacies, prescriptions were transferred to designated receiving pharmacies. This study builds a 41-location evidence ledger from court records, company transfer tables and local reporting, then routes walking and driving trips from 62 census-tract origins to each former pharmacy and its recipient on a fixed 2025 street network. Population-weighted modelled walks rise from 9.0 to 16.0 minutes, with larger increases in higher-poverty tracts. The report also sets out a spatial difference-in-differences design for measuring actual travel and emissions.

Grocery Closures and Neighbourhood Access in Chicago, 2021–2025

Research report · University of Pennsylvania · November 2026

When a grocery store leaves, what remains within reach? This study verifies five closures and one replacement opening (Walmart, Whole Foods and Save A Lot) against USDA SNAP retailer records and food inspections, then compares routed access from the same tract origins before and after each event. Kenwood shows the largest loss, with modelled walks rising from 11.5 to 16.1 minutes, while the Save A Lot opening in Englewood restores the proximity lost when Whole Foods closed.
